# Hardware

Cast or printed metal parts follow the casting floors in the sculpted-metal-casting skill (0.8 to 1.0 mm minimum on any vein, wire or prong; one watertight body per part). On top of those:

## Fit to the leather
- Model every part against the real leather stack at that point (upper plus lining plus any reinforcement), not the bare surface.
- Eyelets and grommets: buy stock ones and a matching setter unless the eyelet itself is the design. A cast eyelet needs a post long enough for the stack plus 1.5 to 2 mm to roll.
- Speed hooks and rivets: post plus a back cap; hole in the leather 0.2 to 0.3 mm over the post.
- Buckles: inside width = strap width plus 1 to 2 mm. Tongue reaches the far bar. Strap holes on a 10 to 15 mm pitch.
- Heel plates and toe guards: screwed or nailed into the sole, so they need countersunk holes and enough metal round each (2 mm minimum).
- Caps over cut leather points (collar tips, strap ends): shape the cap to the point, don't use a round or elliptical cone. Measure the leather's real section at each depth by ray-casting the point along its normal (a fitted surface or interpolation misses by 2 to 3 mm where the point curls), pad 1.7 mm each side and 1.6 mm over the leather face, open the mouth with an inner lip that clears the leather, and rivet once through the cap. Then test both leather faces against the cap section; the stock metal-in-leather check alone can't see leather poking out of a sheath.
- D-rings and strap anchors on bags: wire at least 3 mm for anything carrying weight, closed (cast as a ring, not an open hook).

## Wear
- Anything on a flex line (instep, ankle) must not dig in when the boot flexes 30 degrees.
- No sharp edge facing the wearer or a garment. Points and needles face outward and away from straps and laces.
- Hardware on the inside of the ankle clears the other boot by 10 mm when standing.

## Making it
Print in castable resin and cast (silver, brass, bronze), or direct-print metal through a service. Bought hardware goes on the cutting list with a part number.

## Hardware inside the leather (magnets, stiffeners, weights)
Anything sewn or glued between two layers of leather is hidden. Render it the way it looks in real life: a soft swelling
in the hide plus the stitched ring or box that holds it in place. Never draw it as a visible disc, puck or mesh on the surface.
- Model it as an insert, not a part: kind (magnet, stiffener or weight), size, depth, the piece it sits in and where.
  In a drape job that is the `inserts` list; the renderer makes a shallow dome displacement conformed to the
  draped leather, offset by the leather thickness, plus a stitch ring 2 to 3 mm outside the insert.
- Magnets come in mated pairs: one in each face that meets. Render a magnetic closure closed (the two faces touching)
  unless the view is about opening it.
- Pull tabs on a magnetic placket are short (about 25 x 12 mm) and sit flush with the hem, not hanging below it.
- Swelling height: about 0.4 mm for a 3 mm magnet under 1.9 mm leather. If you can see a hard outline, it is wrong.
- Don't use exposed decorative discs (wood, tsuge, metal) as a stand-in for a hidden closure.

## Tools and contents shown in pockets
A pocket, sleeve, holster or tray cradle made for a named object is shown holding that object.
- Use the real, photo-matched CAD of the object (one named mesh and material per part), never a box or a generic stand-in.
- Place it by the pocket mouth: the part inside the pocket is cut away at the mouth, the rest sits proud by the leather
  thickness. Size the pocket and keeper to the object's measured section plus 2 to 3 mm of ease.
- If the CAD isn't ready, wait for it or leave the pocket empty and say so; don't fake it.
